# Textile75 source audit — preparation only

2026-10-29 UTC. Read-only inspection of the current textiles71 geometry recipe, retained 74 screenshot, original/candidate GLBs and borrowed material images. No Blender process, new world frame, recipe/asset/runtime edit, default adoption or visual acceptance. Only this report and `textile75-source-audit.json` were written. Runtime QA remains the gate before aesthetic implementation.

## Priority findings

1. **Observed pixels:** in `docs/previews/v2-route-craft-38/house-textiles74-candidate-whole-room-0.png` (1280×800), the bed has a long nearly straight thin perimeter; chest blankets read as parallel flat slabs, especially the pale top folded piece. Existing motifs and separate stack layers remain visible. This one view does not establish bench quality, hidden surfaces, new runtime behavior, or the sole shading cause.
2. **Confirmed bed recipe:** `tools/blender/v2_house_textiles62.py:33–89` makes a top and bottom with the same XY, joined by one side strip. Quilt thickness reaches **8 mm at every perimeter vertex**, runner **5–7 mm**; chest cover later uses **4–6 mm**. Mattress has 54 mm perimeter / 113 mm central thickness. The quilt retains 30/19 mm broad ridge terms, but they taper to zero at the boundary. Thus adding more broad center loft alone does not change the straight thin silhouette. It is a contact-conforming height field, not a rounded wrapping edge. This is a source-supported explanation of the appearance, not evidence of new interpenetration.
3. **Confirmed stack geometry:** the four pieces are still original soft27 two-leaf meshes, each 2,940 vertices / 5,872 triangles, rigidly settled by `deform_folded`. Actual GLB top grids vary only **3.00–3.34 mm** over a nominal **840×540 mm** footprint; a front-center leaf is **43.45–43.53 mm thick**. `v2_soft_goods.py:44–48` uses `layer*.50 + side*(.46 + .03*puff)`: substantial constant thickness, very little broad loft. Preserving topology/UV and eliminating float did not remove the inherited slab cross-section.
4. **Confirmed inherited UV limitation:** all four folded pieces have **368/368 near-vertical side triangles with zero UV area**, unchanged between original and current candidate. Each has 1,840/5,872 zero-UV-area triangles including piping, about 13.43–13.45% of geometric surface area. Top/bottom share boundary UVs, and each welt cross-section shares one UV. These surfaces cannot carry independently varying two-dimensional weave in both surface directions. This is not a new71 regression, and does not mean the entire textile has no valid UVs. The newer `solid()` side-strip unwrap fixes the main quilt/cover sides, but does not repair retained folded-source UVs or welt UVs.
5. **Material evidence does not justify a gloss/color workaround:** actual embedded maps are 512²; roughness-channel means are blue/red .950, wool .970, gold/linen .940. Normal scales are .48/.52 and normal-map XY values stay roughly .45–.55 before scaling. Runtime `structures.js:_material` clones the existing PBR maps; there is no cloth-specific sheen extension in these source materials. Fine, restrained normals cannot supply the missing silhouette/volume. The share of apparent hardness caused by these maps versus shape is unmeasured; do not lower roughness, brighten albedo, add light, or change exposure to conceal it.

## Bounded first geometry-only candidate (proposal, not implemented)

Keep the nine-node package, object transforms, source materials/motifs, current UV loops, both folded leaves and both physical welts. Keep full mesh/detail density, collision ownership and explicit OFF rollback. Do not restart the failed per-vertex support-ray warp.

The clearest isolated first comparison is **the four folded pieces plus their already-existing top cover**; freeze the bed/mattress/bench for that comparison. Re-profile the existing leaf grids into a genuinely pinched perimeter and broader soft crown using one smooth coupled deformation per piece. Preserve leaf ordering with an explicit positive separation/thickness constraint; move each welt with its own seam. Follow this with the existing *one rigid support settlement per whole piece*. The cover must follow the revised upper lattice and identical triangle diagonals. No single-shell replacement, new decorative objects, deletion of a layer, or independent displacement of corresponding leaves. Exact original bounds/footprint should remain a tested constraint unless a specific, bounded deviation is explicitly chosen.

Then make a separate bed-only comparison: retain mattress/slat support and the same quilt/runner meshes, but shape a rounded/turned edge through existing perimeter rows and vary the compressed hem continuously rather than increasing center ridges alone. The runner must continue to follow the actual revised quilt; do not independently downfold it into the mattress. Preserve the sewn rim and current motif UVs. Numerical amplitudes should be chosen from close/low-angle comparison, not declared visually correct by this audit.

Required gates: existing source/owner/collision tests; original non-target mesh and material fingerprints; topology/UV/welt identity; positive geometric triangle area; coupled leaf order and triangle-interior self/contact checks; bounded adjacent displacement; actual same-camera whole-room, bed/chest and low-side/edge views. The existing `shape-regression67` **zero displacement-gradient** condition encodes rigid settlement, so a shape candidate needs a separately named smooth-deformation test rather than weakening that historical test or falsely claiming it passed. Changing geometry also invalidates GI73's derived geometry hash: do not reuse/relable those fields; regenerate and validate a matching group before adoption.

### Later UV correction is a separate, explicit change

If strict all-loop UV preservation remains mandatory, leave the inherited side/welt limitation documented. A later targeted correction could preserve all top/bottom motif loops while re-unwrapping only side strips and welt cross-sections with nonzero area and physical scale. That is **not UV-unchanged**, even if node identities, motif images and material slots remain unchanged. It requires separate scope/visual review and normal/roughness mapping checks. Do not silently include it in the first geometry-only comparison.
